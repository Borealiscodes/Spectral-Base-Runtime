## **🌑 Executive Determination**

Across all six case studies, the unified determination is:

> **Operators are pure math.  
> Stability is global.  
> Enforcement is kernel‑mediated.  
> Interventions occur only at boundaries.  
> Spectral continuity is mandatory.  
> Future operators inherit all of the above.**

This doctrine governs the entire stability architecture of Spectral‑Base‑Runtime.

## **🌒 Consolidated Determinations by Case Study**

### **1 — Operator Purity Boundary**  
Operators must remain **mathematically pure**, with:

- no clamping  
- no sanitization  
- no fallback logic  
- no stability flag interpretation  
- no mid‑operator interventions  

Operators = math.  
Stability = policy.  
Kernel = enforcement.

### **2 — Stability Layer Scope**  
The Stability Layer is **global, centralized**, and exclusively responsible for:

- clamping  
- sanitization  
- fallback behaviour  
- SAFE_MODE, STRICT_METRIC, ALLOW_DEGRADED_METRIC  
- spectral‑safe intervention boundaries  

Operators never touch stability logic.

### **3 — Clamping Policy Placement**  
Clamping must be:

- **post‑operator**  
- **global**  
- **kernel‑enforced**  
- **never inside operators**  

Sanitization must be **pre‑operator**.  
Fallback behaviour must be **global**.

### **4 — Spectral Integrity Risk**  
Stability interventions must:

- preserve spectral continuity  
- avoid distorting eigenstructure  
- avoid breaking manifold smoothness  
- avoid mid‑operator perturbations  
- maintain relative spectral structure  

SAFE_MODE must be conservative, not distortive.

### **5 — Runtime Kernel Context Semantics**  
The Runtime Kernel must:

- enforce stability globally  
- maintain unified stability context  
- provide pre/post operator hooks  
- isolate operators from stability flags  
- guarantee spectral continuity  
- ensure deterministic operator execution  

The kernel becomes a governance engine.

### **6 — Operator Inheritance Model**  
All future operators inherit:

- purity  
- stability isolation  
- spectral continuity constraints  
- kernel‑mediated enforcement  
- deterministic semantics  
- zero access to stability flags  

This prevents drift and fragmentation.
